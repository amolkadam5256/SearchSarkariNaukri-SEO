## 1. What Is a Gazetted Officer?

A Gazetted Officer is an officer whose government appointment, post or status is recognised through the applicable official Gazette and service rules. The Gazette of India and State Gazettes are official government publications used to notify legally recognised government decisions, appointments, rules and orders.

In simple terms, a gazetted officer is not just any government employee. The post must have gazetted status under the relevant service classification. This distinction matters for recruitment, administrative authority, document attestation, public dealing and career planning.

Candidates looking for officer-level government jobs can start from [Latest Government Jobs](https://www.searchsarkarinaukri.com/jobs), but the gazetted status of any vacancy should always be checked in the official notification before applying.

## 2. Gazetted Officer Meaning in Simple Words

Gazetted Officer meaning in simple words: a Gazetted Officer is a government officer whose official position is recognised through a Gazette notification or applicable service rules. Such officers may perform official duties such as supervision, administration, record approval, technical decisions, inspection or certification when their department authorises it.

The phrase does not mean unlimited authority. A Gazetted Officer can exercise only the powers attached to the officer's post, department, jurisdiction and delegation of powers.

## 3. Who Is a Gazetted Officer in India?

A Gazetted Officer in India is a government officer holding a post that is classified as gazetted under the relevant rules. Common examples may include officers from All India Services, central services, state civil services, revenue services, police services, government medical services, government engineering services, academic services, defence appointments and selected specialist cadres.

However, the examples must be read carefully. A job title alone does not prove gazetted status. Some government medical, engineering or academic posts may be gazetted, while another post with a similar title may not be gazetted under its service rules.

For central government recruitment, candidates should verify officer posts through [UPSC Recruitment](https://www.searchsarkarinaukri.com/department/upsc). Maharashtra candidates should also track MPSC-related recruitment pages and official MPSC notices where state service classification is mentioned.

## 4. Is Every Government Officer a Gazetted Officer?

No, every government officer is not automatically a Gazetted Officer. Gazetted status depends on the classification of the post and the applicable service rules. A person may work in government, hold a responsible post, or use an officer designation, but that alone does not prove gazetted status.

Always check the official notification, service rules, appointment order or Gazette reference rather than relying only on the job title.

## 5. List of Gazetted Officer Posts in India

## 6. Types of Gazetted Officers

Gazetted officers are generally discussed under Group A and Group B classifications. Group A usually contains higher-level administrative, executive, technical, medical, engineering, scientific, academic and specialist posts. Group B can include both gazetted and non-gazetted posts.

The exact type depends on whether the post belongs to central government, state government, defence, police, education, health, public sector administration or another notified service.

## 7. Group A Gazetted Officers

Group A gazetted officers usually hold higher administrative, technical, specialist or supervisory responsibilities. Many Group A posts are treated as gazetted, especially in central and state services, but candidates should still verify the specific post classification from the relevant service rules.

Common Group A routes include UPSC examinations, State Public Service Commission examinations, specialist recruitment and departmental promotion. ## 8. Are All Group A Officers Gazetted?

Group A services commonly include gazetted officer posts, but the correct approach is to verify the classification from the applicable rules instead of assuming solely from the group label. Recruitment notifications and service rules usually mention group, pay level, classification and whether the post is gazetted or non-gazetted.

This careful wording avoids the common overstatement that all Group A officers are gazetted in every context.

## 9. Group B Gazetted Officers

Some Group B officers are gazetted, while some Group B employees are non-gazetted. Whether a Group B post is gazetted depends on the department's service rules and government notification.

This distinction is important for MPSC, state services, technical services and departmental promotion posts. For example, a candidate may see Group B in a recruitment notice and still need to check whether it is Group B Gazetted or Group B Non-Gazetted.

## 10. Are All Group B Officers Gazetted?

No, all Group B officers are not gazetted. Group B includes both gazetted and non-gazetted posts. The exact classification depends on the recruitment rules, service rules, government notification and the wording of the official advertisement.

For Maharashtra recruitment, candidates should pay special attention to terms such as Group A, Group B Gazetted and Group B Non-Gazetted in MPSC notices.

## 11. Gazetted Officer vs Non-Gazetted Officer

The difference between Gazetted Officer and Non-Gazetted Officer is mainly based on service classification, official notification status and delegated authority. Both are government employees, but their appointment status and powers are not the same.

| Feature | Gazetted Officer | Non-Gazetted Officer |

| Gazette link | Appointment/post/status may be notified through the official Gazette or relevant rules | Usually does not carry the same gazetted notification status |
| Service group | Commonly Group A and selected Group B posts | Can include different service groups and support/operational roles |
| Authority | Depends on post, department and delegation | Depends on post and department |
| Document attestation | May attest only where authorised or accepted by the form/rules | May attest only if a specific rule separately authorises it |
| Examples | IAS, IPS, IRS, state service officers, medical/engineering/academic posts where notified | Clerical, technical, assistant, operational and other non-gazetted posts |

## 12. Powers, Duties and Responsibilities of a Gazetted Officer

The powers of a Gazetted Officer depend on the officer's post, department, jurisdiction and delegated authority. A Gazetted Officer does not have unlimited power merely because the post is gazetted.

Common responsibilities may include administration, supervision, departmental approvals, technical decision-making, public service delivery, record verification, inspection, certification where authorised, official correspondence, staff supervision and implementation of government rules or schemes.

For government-job applicants, this matters because different officer posts have different work profiles. UPSC, MPSC, engineering, medical, police, revenue and education roles may all involve different powers even when some are gazetted.

## 13. Can a Gazetted Officer Attest Documents?

A Gazetted Officer can attest documents only where the application form, institution, department or rule accepts or requires attestation by an authorised gazetted officer. Attestation usually means the officer compares the photocopy with the original document and signs or stamps it as verified.

Candidates should not assume that every Gazetted Officer can attest every document for every purpose. Some forms now require self-attestation. Some require verification by a particular authority. Some may accept a notary, institution head or department-specific officer instead.

## 17. Other Recruitment and Promotion Routes

Not every Gazetted Officer becomes one through a major civil services exam. Some gazetted posts are filled through specialist recruitment, direct department recruitment, campus-linked recruitment, statutory bodies, autonomous institutions or promotion from lower service levels.

Examples can include medical officers, engineering officers, research officers, law officers, education officers, professors, scientists and administrative officers where the post is classified as gazetted. Departmental promotion is also an important route for existing government employees who become eligible for higher gazetted posts under promotion rules.

## 19. Gazetted Officer Salary and Pay Level

Gazetted Officer salary in India depends on the post, pay level, service, department, seniority, location, allowances and promotion rules. Group A posts generally start at higher pay levels than many Group B posts, but salary should not be guessed from the term gazetted alone.

A typical official notification may mention pay level, basic pay range, allowances such as DA, HRA and TA, probation rules, promotion path and posting location. Do not rely on social-media salary claims or unsourced salary tables. Use the official notification and pay matrix details as the final source.

## 20. How to Verify Whether a Post Is Gazetted

To verify whether a post is gazetted, check the official recruitment notification, service rules, appointment order or Gazette notification for the exact post and department. Do not rely only on job title, salary, seniority or online lists.

Verification steps:

1. Identify the exact post name.
2. Identify the department, cadre and service.
3. Check the official recruitment advertisement.
4. Look for words such as Group A, Group B, Gazetted, Non-Gazetted, Ministerial or Non-Ministerial.
5. Check the applicable service rules or department notification.
6. Search the official Gazette source where relevant.
7. Confirm from the official department, UPSC, State PSC or recruitment authority.

## 22. Can Doctors, Engineers and Professors Be Gazetted Officers?

Doctors, engineers and professors can be Gazetted Officers only when their government post is classified as gazetted under the applicable service rules. The profession alone does not decide gazetted status.

For example, certain government medical officer posts, government engineering posts and government academic posts may be gazetted. But not every doctor, engineer or professor working with a public institution should automatically be described as a Gazetted Officer without verifying the exact post.
