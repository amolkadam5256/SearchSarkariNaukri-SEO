India's PMO Has a New Address

India's Prime Minister's Office now operates from Seva Teerth, a new administrative complex in New Delhi, rather than the historic South Block. For decades, South Block and North Block on Raisina Hill were synonymous with the machinery of India's central government. That changed with the formal inauguration of Seva Teerth in February 2026.

The PMO, the Cabinet Secretariat, and the National Security Council Secretariat are now housed in Seva Teerth, while several major ministries have moved into the adjoining Kartavya Bhavan buildings. Read more about how the wider machinery of government works in our guide to India's district administration.

What Was South Block?

South Block is one of the most recognisable government buildings in New Delhi, having historically housed the Prime Minister's Office and the Ministry of Defence. For generations, it stood as the visible face of India's central administration.

Why Was a New Complex Needed?

The government has described the move as an effort to build a more modern, efficient, and citizen-centric administrative environment — not simply replace an old building with a new one. Modern administration requires infrastructure the older buildings weren't designed for:

  • Digital systems
  • Secure communications
  • Large-scale inter-ministry coordination
  • Energy-efficient infrastructure
  • Improved accessibility

What Is Seva Teerth?

Seva Teerth houses three of the most important institutions in the Indian government:

PMOPrime Minister's Office
NSCSNational Security Council Secretariat
Cabinet SecretariatCentral coordinating body

The name itself is significant — "Seva" means service, and "Teerth" traditionally refers to a sacred or important place.

What Is Kartavya Bhavan?

Kartavya Bhavan is the adjoining government-office complex, housing ministries including Finance, Defence, Health and Family Welfare, and Education. See related coverage in our explainer on the Cabinet Secretary's role.

Is South Block Gone?

No — South Block still stands. The move changed where key government offices operate, not the physical buildings themselves, which retain their historical and architectural significance.

Why Does This Matter to Citizens?

Government buildings shape how departments coordinate, how secure information is handled, and how administration is organised. Better infrastructure for ministries covering finance, defence, health, and education can affect how decisions actually get implemented — though the real test is whether the new infrastructure improves how government works, not just where it's located.

From Colonial Buildings to Modern Administration

At the inauguration, the government framed Seva Teerth and Kartavya Bhavan as part of a shift away from colonial-era administrative structures toward citizen-centric governance. Whichever way one reads that framing, the physical shift is real: some of India's most powerful offices now operate from a complex built for a digital-era government rather than the monumental buildings of Raisina Hill.
