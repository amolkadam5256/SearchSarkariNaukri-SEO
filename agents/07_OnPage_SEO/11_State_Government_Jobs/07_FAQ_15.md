## 1. What are state government jobs?

**Answer:**  
State government jobs (also referred to as state *Sarkari Naukri*) are public sector employment opportunities administered directly by the government of an Indian state or Union Territory. Unlike Central Government posts—which fall under the Government of India and can involve nationwide postings—state government posts are organized under state administrative cadres. Positions span gazetted administrative services (Deputy Collectors, DSPs, Tehsildars recruited via State Public Service Commissions), subordinate departmental staff (revenue patwaris, police constables, clerks), technical cadres (Junior Engineers in State PWD or Irrigation), healthcare personnel (nurses, medical officers), and teachers in state-run schools. Their pay scales, allowances, pension schemes, and service rules are established by the respective State Pay Commissions or adapted Seven Central Pay Commission resolutions.

## 3. Can I apply for another state's government jobs?

**Answer:**  
In most cases, yes—citizens of India can apply for state government jobs in another state under the **General / Unreserved (UR)** category, in accordance with the constitutional right to equality of opportunity under Article 16. However, conditions apply:
1. **No Category Reservation:** Out-of-state candidates generally cannot claim SC, ST, OBC, or EWS reservation benefits; category reservations are almost universally restricted to candidates holding a caste certificate issued by the hiring state.
2. **Local Language Proficiency:** Many posts require candidates to have studied the regional official language (e.g., Marathi in Maharashtra, Gujarati in Gujarat) in Class 10/12 or pass a qualifying language test.
3. **Domicile-Restricted Posts:** Certain non-gazetted, clerical, village-level, or police constable posts are legally restricted to local state residents via state domicile rules. Always inspect the "Nationality and Domicile" clause in the official notification.

## 4. Is domicile compulsory for state government jobs?

**Answer:**  
Domicile is **not universally compulsory** for every state government job, but its requirement depends on the post grade and recruitment rules:
- **State PSC Civil Services & Technical Posts:** Open to eligible Indian citizens across states under the Unreserved category, though General cutoffs apply.
- **Group C & Group D Posts / Local Cadres:** Often legally mandate a **Domicile or Permanent Resident Certificate (PRC)** issued by an authorized Tehsildar or Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) of that specific state.
- **Reservation & Fee Concessions:** Even where domicile is not required for basic eligibility, it is universally mandatory if you wish to claim age relaxation, fee exemptions, or caste reservation (SC/ST/OBC/EWS). Always review the official notification rather than assuming a universal domicile rule.

## 5. What is the age limit for state government jobs?

**Answer:**  
Age criteria vary widely by state, recruiting department, and post level. While central recruitments often cap general age at 30 or 32 years, state governments generally provide broader windows:
- **Standard Minimum Age:** Typically 18 years for Group C/D and police posts, and 21 years for State PSC gazetted posts.
- **Standard Maximum Age (General):** Ranges between 36 and 40 years in states like Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, and Rajasthan, and up to 42–44 years in Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, and West Bengal.
- **Statutory Age Relaxations:** 
  - Scheduled Castes (SC) / Scheduled Tribes (ST): +5 years
  - Other Backward Classes (OBC/NCL): +3 years
  - Persons with Benchmark Disabilities (PwBD): +10 years
  - Ex-Servicemen: Deduct military service + 3 years
  - State-specific relaxations exist for women candidates, home guards, and state government employees.  
Candidates should verify their exact cutoff date using the **[SearchSarkariNaukri Age Calculator](/age-calculator)**.

## 10. Are state government job applications online or offline?

**Answer:**  
The vast majority (over 95%) of state government recruitment applications are conducted entirely **online** through dedicated recruitment portals or State PSC One-Time Registration (OTR) platforms. Online applications ensure transparency, immediate fee confirmation, and automated admit card generation. However, offline applications—where candidates must print a prescribed proforma, attach attested photocopies of certificates, and send them via registered/speed post—are still occasionally used for specialized contract appointments, walk-in medical officer drives, apprenticeship positions in state transport workshops, or judicial clerkships in district courts. Always verify the application mode in the official advertisement.

## 13. What is the difference between State PSC and department recruitment?

**Answer:**  
- **State Public Service Commissions (PSCs):** Constitutional bodies established under Article 315 of the Constitution. They recruit primarily for gazetted Class 1 (Group A) and Class 2 (Group B) executive cadres—such as civil services, police leadership, judicial officers, and assistant engineers—through structured multi-stage examinations (Prelims, Mains, and Interviews).
- **Departmental Recruitment:** Conducted directly by specific government departments, autonomous boards, or subordinate service selection boards (e.g., Police Recruitment Boards, State Health Societies, Zilla Parishads). They hire for non-gazetted Group C and Group D positions (constables, nurses, technicians, clerks) using single-tier written exams, skill tests, or physical endurance evaluations.

## 14. Are state government jobs transferable?

**Answer:**  
Yes, but transfers are strictly confined to the jurisdiction of the hiring state cadre:
- **Statewide Cadres:** Officers recruited via State PSCs (DSPs, Deputy Collectors, Tehsildars, Sales Tax Inspectors) are subject to periodic transfers every 3 years across different districts, divisions, and commissionerates within the state.
- **District / Local Cadres:** Positions recruited at the Zilla Parishad, district court, or municipal corporation level (such as Talathis, Shikshan Sevaks, Arogya Sevaks, and civic engineers) are generally non-transferable outside their respective district or civic corporation jurisdiction, providing lifelong regional posting stability.
- **Inter-State Transfers:** State government employees cannot be transferred to another state or central government department, except in rare cases of deputation approved by both state governments.
