## What is the SSC CGL examination?

SSC CGL is a national competitive examination used by SSC to recruit graduates to listed Group B and Group C vacancies in participating central ministries, departments, organizations and statutory bodies. The 2026 notice lists posts across different pay levels and includes post-specific age and qualification conditions.

The examination has two computer-based tiers. Tier I is used to shortlist candidates for Tier II. The papers a candidate must take in Tier II, qualifying modules and the merit calculation can differ according to the post. The current notice and its amendments determine the rules for a particular cycle.

### Why candidates consider SSC CGL

- It provides access to a range of central government job profiles.
- Posts span administration, taxation, audit, accounts, statistics, investigation and related functions.
- Posts are assigned different pay levels and may have different eligibility or skill requirements.
- A candidate can express post preferences under the process and deadlines stated by SSC; allocation depends on merit, preferences, vacancies and eligibility.

A bachelor’s degree from a recognized university is required for most posts. The 2026 notice specifies additional conditions for statistical posts, including the Junior Statistical Officer and Statistical Investigator Grade-II. Applicants must meet the notice’s qualification cut-off date; final-year status alone does not establish eligibility.

## SSC CGL exam pattern 2026

The [2026 SSC notice](https://ssc.gov.in/api/attachment/uploads/masterData/NoticeBoards/Notice_of_adv_cgl_2026.pdf) sets a two-tier computer-based examination. Tier I has four sections and 100 multiple-choice questions for 200 marks, with 60 minutes total and sectional timers. Tier II Paper I is compulsory for all posts; Papers II and III apply to candidates shortlisted for specified statistical and Assistant Audit/Accounts posts.

### Tier I

| Subject | Questions | Marks |
|---|---:|---:|
| General Intelligence and Reasoning | 25 | 50 |
| General Awareness | 25 | 50 |
| Quantitative Aptitude | 25 | 50 |
| English Comprehension | 25 | 50 |
| **Total** | **100** | **200** |

The notice gives 60 minutes with a 15-minute sectional timer for each subject. It specifies 0.50 marks deducted for each wrong answer. Candidates eligible for a scribe/assistive technology under the notice receive compensatory time of 20 minutes per exam hour; the Tier I total and section timers are extended accordingly. Show the normal and eligible-candidate timings separately and link to the exact eligibility rules rather than merging them into one timer.

### Tier II

Tier II includes the following papers and modules under the 2026 notice. SSC may conduct Paper I, Paper II and Paper III in separate shifts or on separate days:

- **Paper I (compulsory):** Mathematical Abilities; Reasoning and General Intelligence; English Language and Comprehension; General Awareness; Computer Knowledge Test; Data Entry Speed Test (DEST).
- **Paper II:** Statistics, for eligible candidates shortlisted for the specified JSO / Statistical Investigator posts.
- **Paper III:** General Studies (Finance and Economics), for candidates shortlisted for Assistant Audit Officer / Assistant Accounts Officer posts.

Paper I is compulsory. Under the 2026 notice it is conducted in two sessions on the same day. Paper II is for candidates who applied for and are shortlisted for the specified statistical posts; Paper III is for candidates shortlisted for Assistant Audit Officer / Assistant Accounts Officer posts. The section-wise structure below is transcribed from the notice; keep the SSC PDF beside this information because SSC can amend it.

| Paper I module | Questions | Maximum marks | Time shown in notice | Wrong-answer penalty |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Section I, Part A: Mathematical Abilities | 30 | 90 | 1 hour for Section I (30 minutes per part) | 1 mark |
| Section I, Part B: Reasoning and General Intelligence | 30 | 90 | Included above | 1 mark |
| Section II, Part A: English Language and Comprehension | 45 | 135 | 1 hour for Section II (40 minutes for English; 20 minutes for General Awareness) | 1 mark |
| Section II, Part B: General Awareness | 25 | 75 | Included above | 1 mark |
| Section III: Computer Knowledge Test | 20 | 60 | 15 minutes | 1 mark |
| Section IV: Data Entry Speed Test (DEST) | One data-entry task | Qualifying | 15 minutes | Not scored as an MCQ paper |

Sections I and II together contain 130 questions for 390 marks and run for 2 hours. With the 15-minute Computer Knowledge Test, Session I totals 2 hours 15 minutes. DEST is in Session II for 15 minutes. The Computer Knowledge Test is mandatory for all candidates and qualifying; DEST is qualifying and mandatory except for exemptions explicitly allowed by the notice. SSC may apply higher standards for specified posts. The notice describes a passage of about 2,000 key depressions administered over 15 minutes; this is not a promise that every candidate must reach a keystroke-rate target. DEST is evaluated against the notice’s error limits and applicable post standards/exemptions. Verify the current rules in the [official 2026 notice](https://ssc.gov.in/api/attachment/uploads/masterData/NoticeBoards/Notice_of_adv_cgl_2026.pdf) before each cycle.

| Additional Tier II paper | Applicable candidates | Questions | Marks | Duration | Wrong-answer penalty |

Paper II and Paper III are separate papers for the eligible/shortlisted candidates specified by SSC. Do not imply that all CGL applicants take both. The current notice controls shortlisting, qualifying standards, scribe/compensatory-time provisions and final merit rules.

**Accessible timing note:** For eligible candidates covered by the notice’s scribe/compensatory-time provisions, Tier II timing is extended: Section I 1 hour 20 minutes (40 minutes per part), Section II 1 hour 20 minutes (55 minutes English and 25 minutes General Awareness), Computer Knowledge Test 20 minutes, DEST 20 minutes, and Papers II/III 2 hours 40 minutes. ## SSC CGL syllabus 2026

The syllabus below is a concise navigation summary, not a substitute for the official indicative syllabus. Let users expand subject groups and link every topic explorer to the notice and official question papers.

### Tier I subjects

**General Intelligence and Reasoning:** analogy, classification, series, coding and decoding, problem solving, relationship concepts, spatial orientation, Venn diagrams, inference, verbal and non-verbal reasoning, and other topics in the notice.

**General Awareness:** current events and general awareness, with topics concerning India and neighboring countries, history, culture, geography, economic scene, general policy and scientific research.

**Quantitative Aptitude:** number systems, arithmetic, ratio and proportion, averages, interest, profit and loss, time and work, algebra, geometry, mensuration, trigonometry, heights and distances, and charts/graphs.

**English Comprehension:** English comprehension and language ability as described in the notice.

### Tier II Paper I

- **Mathematical Abilities:** number systems, arithmetic, algebra, geometry, mensuration, trigonometry, statistics and probability.
- **Reasoning and General Intelligence:** verbal and non-verbal reasoning, analogies, classification, series, coding, Venn diagrams, problem solving and critical thinking.
- **English Language and Comprehension:** vocabulary, grammar, sentence and passage comprehension, and other topics in the notice.
- **General Awareness:** general awareness and current events as described by SSC.
- **Computer Knowledge Test:** basic computer and digital knowledge listed in the official syllabus.
- **DEST:** data-entry skill test; qualifying rules and applicable standards are post-specific.

### Tier II Papers II and III

- **Paper II — Statistics:** data collection/presentation, central tendency, dispersion, moments/skewness/kurtosis, correlation/regression, probability, random variables/distributions, sampling, statistical inference, analysis of variance, time series and index numbers. It is for candidates who applied for and are shortlisted to the specified JSO / Statistical Investigator Grade-II posts.
- **Paper III — General Studies (Finance and Economics):** Finance and Accounts (80 marks) covers accounting scope and conventions, single/double entry, journals, ledgers, trial balance, error correction, final accounts, capital/revenue expenditure, depreciation, inventory, non-profit accounts, bills of exchange and self-balancing ledgers. Economics and Governance (120 marks) covers CAG, Finance Commission, microeconomics, demand/supply and elasticity, consumer behavior, production/cost, market forms, Indian sectors/national income/population/poverty/unemployment/infrastructure, post-1991 reforms, RBI and banking, monetary/fiscal policy, budget/deficits/balance of payments, the FRBM Act and IT in governance. It is for candidates shortlisted for Assistant Audit Officer / Assistant Accounts Officer posts.

The full Tier II syllabus is in sections 13.11.1–13.11.7 of the [official 2026 notice](https://ssc.gov.in/api/attachment/uploads/masterData/Notice_of_adv_cgl_2026.pdf). The official notice is authoritative for topic wording, depth and applicable paper. Do not fabricate predicted weightage, “most important” scores or topic frequencies without a transparent dataset and method.

## SSC CGL selection process

1. Submit one valid online application within the notice window.
2. Appear in Tier I; meet the minimum qualifying requirement and SSC’s separate category-wise shortlisting cutoff for the relevant post group. Minimum qualifying marks are not the same as the competitive cutoff.
3. Appear in Tier II papers applicable to the post(s) for which shortlisted.
4. Qualify mandatory Computer Knowledge and DEST sections, and any higher post-specific standards that apply.
5. Complete document verification and other post-specific formalities through the designated authority. Selected posts may require physical/medical checks after final selection and nomination.
6. Final merit and tentative allocation are based on Tier II performance, applicable qualifying conditions and candidate post preferences under the notice.

### Minimum qualifying marks and DEST standards (2026)

The 2026 notice gives the following minimum qualifying percentages for Tier I and the applicable scored Tier II sections/papers. These are eligibility floors, not a promise of shortlisting or selection; actual cutoffs can be higher and are fixed separately by SSC.

| Category | Minimum qualifying percentage |
|---|---:|
| UR | 30% |
| OBC / EWS | 25% |
| All other categories | 20% |

For DEST, the notice gives maximum error percentages (minimum qualifying standards): UR 20%, OBC/EWS 25%, and all other categories 30%. DEST and the Computer Knowledge Test are qualifying modules; SSC may require higher standards for specified posts. Read the notice for exemptions and precise post applicability.

**Merit clarification:** The 2026 notice states that final merit/selection is based on Tier II performance, with the paper combination depending on the post group and qualifying conditions. Tier I is used for shortlisting. Candidates must submit post preferences when SSC opens the option form; preferences are treated as final under the notice. Post allocation also depends on merit, reported vacancies, eligibility and the rules for the relevant post.

There is no interview in the 2026 CGL scheme. Final selection depends on the current notice and all required conditions; a Tier I result or qualifying module alone does not guarantee appointment.

## SSC CGL preparation roadmap

This is a planning framework, not a selection guarantee. Candidates should adapt it to their available study time and the current syllabus.

### Phase 1: Map the exam

Read the current notice, identify target post requirements and map each Tier I / Tier II topic to a diagnostic question set. Mark post-specific papers and qualifying modules that apply to your target choices.

### Phase 2: Build foundations

Study core concepts in mathematics, reasoning, English and general awareness. Keep a short error log that records the topic, error type and correction. Review concepts before increasing speed.

### Phase 3: Practice under section timing

Because Tier I and Tier II use sectional timing, practice with the actual section order and timed blocks. Record accuracy, time spent and unanswered questions. Use official previous-year papers where available.

### Phase 4: Add post-specific Tier II work

Prepare Statistics or Finance and Economics only if the corresponding post and Tier II paper are relevant to your application strategy. Practice Computer Knowledge and DEST if applicable under the notice.

### Phase 5: Review and simulate

Take full-length mocks using current conditions, review every incorrect or guessed response, and revise weak areas. Mock scores are study indicators, not predictions of official cut-offs or selection.

### Optional 90-day plan

This is a flexible framework for candidates who have about three months. It is not an official SSC schedule and should be adjusted to the candidate’s baseline and the announced exam date.

| Days | Main focus | Weekly checkpoint |

## Frequently asked questions

### What is SSC CGL?

SSC CGL is the Staff Selection Commission’s graduate-level examination for recruitment to listed Group B and Group C posts in central government ministries, departments and organizations. Read the [current SSC notice](https://ssc.gov.in/api/attachment/uploads/masterData/NoticeBoards/Notice_of_adv_cgl_2026.pdf) for cycle-specific rules. The posts and conditions vary by cycle and the current official notice controls.

### What qualification is required for SSC CGL?

Most posts require a bachelor’s degree, but the notice includes specific subject or marks conditions for some statistical posts. Candidates must meet the qualification date and other requirements stated for the post they choose.

### Can final-year students apply for SSC CGL?

The 2026 notice allowed final-year candidates to apply, but they had to possess the required qualification by 1 August 2026. Being enrolled or having a result still pending after that date did not establish eligibility. Check the exact qualification date in each new notice.

### What is the SSC CGL age limit?

There is no single age limit for every post. The 2026 notice lists post-specific brackets including 18–27, 18–30, 18–32 and 20–30 years. The crucial date and applicable relaxation rules must be checked in the notice.

### Is SSC CGL conducted online?

Yes. The 2026 notification describes Tier I and Tier II computer-based examinations. The current notice determines each paper’s timing, modules and qualifying requirements.

### How many tiers are there in SSC CGL?

The 2026 examination has Tier I and Tier II. Tier II contains Paper I and post-specific Paper II or Paper III for candidates shortlisted for specified posts.

### What is the SSC CGL salary?

Posts in the 2026 notice span pay levels 4 to 8, with different basic-pay ranges. Gross and take-home amounts depend on applicable allowances, posting and deductions; one salary figure should not be presented as universal.

### Which SSC CGL post has the highest salary?

The 2026 notice includes pay-level 8 posts, which have a higher basic-pay range than the listed lower pay levels. Vacancies, eligibility, department and post names must be confirmed from the applicable notice; pay level alone is not a complete career comparison.

### Is there negative marking in SSC CGL?

The 2026 notice specifies 0.50 marks for a wrong answer in Tier I. Tier II penalties differ by section and paper, so candidates should check the current scheme before attempting questions.

### Does SSC CGL have an interview?

The 2026 scheme does not include an interview. Selection is based on the examination scheme, qualifying conditions, merit, preferences and verification described in the notice.

### Where can I check SSC CGL results and notices?

Use the official SSC website, notice board, results and answer-key sections. Confirm that the notice says Combined Graduate Level and matches the correct year and stage.

### Is SearchSarkariNaukri the official SSC website?

No. SearchSarkariNaukri is an independent information resource. Candidates should use SSC’s official website for notices, applications, answer keys, admit cards and results.

### How does normalization work in SSC CGL?

When a computer-based examination is held in multiple shifts, SSC applies its published normalization procedure. The normalized scores are used for merit and cut-off decisions as specified in the notification. Do not use an unofficial calculator to predict a final normalized score.

### Is SSC CGL difficult?

Difficulty depends on a candidate’s starting level, preparation time and strengths across the tested subjects. Review the official syllabus and try a timed previous-year paper to identify gaps; no page can promise selection based on a preparation schedule.

### Can I choose my preferred post?

Candidates submit post preferences according to SSC’s instructions. Allocation depends on merit, preferences, vacancies and post-specific eligibility under the applicable notice; preference does not guarantee a particular post.

### Can I apply for more than one SSC examination?

Candidates can apply to separate SSC examinations when their eligibility and each notice’s rules permit it. Check exam dates, application instructions and any restrictions for each examination independently.

### Where can I download the SSC CGL syllabus?

Use the syllabus and scheme included in the current SSC CGL notice. The summary on this page is for navigation and should be checked against the official syllabus before preparation.

### How many tentative vacancies are there in SSC CGL 2026?

SSC’s vacancy list dated 24 September 2026 shows 10,731 tentative vacancies. This is later than the approximately 12,256 vacancies mentioned in the original May notice; vacancies are tentative and may change. Check the [official vacancy PDF](https://ssc.gov.in/api/attachment/uploads/masterData/NoticeBoards/Tentative_vacancy_CGLE2026_24092026.pdf) for post and category rows.

### What are the minimum qualifying marks for SSC CGL 2026?

The 2026 notice lists 30% for UR, 25% for OBC/EWS and 20% for other categories for the specified exam components. These minimums are not the same as the cutoff to qualify for a later stage or receive a post; SSC sets separate cutoffs.

### What is the SSC CGL Tier II DEST requirement?

The 2026 notice describes a 15-minute data-entry passage of about 2,000 key depressions. DEST is qualifying; maximum permitted error percentages differ by category, and some posts require higher standards. The passage length is not an independently guaranteed typing-speed threshold. Check the notice for exemptions and post-specific rules.

### What is the difference between SSC CGL cutoff and minimum qualifying marks?

Minimum qualifying marks are the base percentage stated in the notice. A cutoff is the score SSC actually uses for shortlisting or allocation under the relevant year, category, post group and stage. Meeting the minimum alone does not guarantee progression or selection.

### Do SSC CGL posts have physical or medical tests?

Some posts have post-specific physical, medical or computer proficiency standards. The CGL notice lists the affected posts and requirements; for certain physical/medical standards, the user department conducts checks after final selection and nomination. Check the relevant annexure before choosing post preferences.

### Can beginners prepare for SSC CGL?

Yes. Start by reading the current notice and taking a timed diagnostic paper, then use the syllabus checklist and an error log to plan study. The sample timetable on this page is a flexible example, not a selection guarantee or an official SSC plan.
